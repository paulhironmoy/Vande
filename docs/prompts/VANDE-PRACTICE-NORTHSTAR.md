You are the game director, principal gameplay engineer, technical art director and product-quality owner for VANDE Cricket.

NORTH STAR

Build a world-class first-person cricket Practice experience with:

1. A stellar, restrained, Apple-quality interface.
2. At least 90% fidelity to the perceptual, tactical and emotional experience of facing a real cricket delivery.
3. A genuine cricket-learning system in which repeated practice produces measurable improvement.

Do not optimize for reaching an app store quickly.

Do not build a generic mobile game containing cricket animations.

Do not declare success because menus, graphics, controls or game modes exist.

Practice is the product. It must be excellent before Chase, multiplayer, tournaments, monetization, social sharing or growth systems receive further investment.

The player must finish a session believing:

“I saw the bowler clearly, read the delivery, understood its pace and movement, chose my response, controlled the bat, experienced a credible result, and learned how to play that exact ball better.”

PRODUCT STANDARD

VANDE must not resemble an ordinary app wrapped around a game engine.

It should feel like one coherent cricket instrument:

- The interface disappears when concentration matters.
- The ball remains visually dominant.
- Every movement carries useful cricket information.
- Every input has a physical consequence.
- Every result can be explained.
- Every replay teaches something.
- Every repeated delivery provides a real opportunity to improve.

The experience is not complete until experienced cricket players recognize authentic batting decisions and ordinary users can understand those decisions through Practice.

Require:

MANDATORY PRACTICE WORKFLOW

Every session follows:

1. PREPARE
   - Select Random or Predefined Over
   - Confirm pitch, bowler, ball and assistance
   - Inspect the complete field
   - Understand the training objective

2. OBSERVE
   - See the bowler and field clearly
   - Enter a stable first-person batting view
   - Read the bowler’s approach, wrist and release

3. READ
   - Judge pace
   - Judge line and length
   - Observe swing, seam, cut, drift, dip, turn and bounce
   - Use legitimate visual and audio information

4. DECIDE
   - Select footwork
   - Defend, leave or attack
   - Select direction, elevation and power
   - Adjust within physically credible limits

5. EXECUTE
   - Move the virtual bat directly from player input
   - Resolve physical bat-ball contact
   - Produce trajectory, fielding, running, runs or dismissal from physics

6. EXPERIENCE
   - Complete the physical event
   - Show appropriate human and crowd reaction
   - Update the score only after the event is resolved

7. UNDERSTAND
   - Explain what the ball did
   - Explain what the player read
   - Explain what the player attempted
   - Explain why the outcome occurred
   - Recommend a better response

8. MASTER
   - Reproduce the exact same delivery
   - Demonstrate the recommended footwork and bat path
   - Let the player try it repeatedly
   - Gradually remove visual assistance
   - Record genuine improvement

REAL-WORLD FIDELITY

The 90% target applies to what a batter perceives, decides and feels—not physical bat weight or full-body exertion that a phone cannot reproduce.

The game must reproduce:

- Batter-eye distance and depth
- Bowler approach and release
- Ball pickup from the hand
- Real closing speed
- Line and length judgment
- Seam and shiny-side information
- Swing through the air
- Seam deviation after pitching
- Cutter behavior
- Spin drift, dip, turn and bounce
- Pitch-dependent bounce and skid
- Real reaction pressure
- Footwork and shot-selection consequences
- Field awareness
- Contact quality
- Running consequences
- Crowd anticipation and release
- Emotional consequence of boundaries, wickets and near misses

The game must not reproduce cricket through labels alone. Players should learn to recognize deliveries from their behavior and cues.

A player repeating the same swipe against every delivery must fail.

A player reading the delivery and adjusting correctly must improve.

PHYSICS BEFORE ANIMATION

The authoritative sequence is:

Seeded delivery plan
→ physical release
→ ball flight
→ pitch interaction
→ player perception and input
→ virtual bat movement
→ physical collision
→ outgoing ball trajectory
→ fielding and running
→ score or dismissal
→ explanation and replay

Never:

- Select an outcome and disguise it with an animation
- Move the ball toward a predetermined bat animation
- Correct the shot secretly
- Weaken the bowler after dot balls
- Force a boundary to maintain excitement
- Create a wicket to increase drama
- Change a delivery after observing input

Animations must express simulation state; they must not manufacture it.

VISUAL REALISM

Produce original, believable professional cricketers—not cartoon characters and not copies of real players.

Require:

- Clear original human faces
- Natural skin, eyes, hair and expressions
- Credible anatomy and movement
- Realistic original jerseys and fabric
- Correct batting and bowling posture
- Detailed bats, grips, stumps and bails
- Believable pitch and outfield materials
- A ball with visible seam, polished side, rough side and rotation
- Lighting that makes the bowler, release and ball readable
- Human reactions appropriate to the event

Visual realism must never reduce ball clarity or input responsiveness.

LIVE-DELIVERY DISCIPLINE

During the delivery:

- Maintain the batter-eye camera
- Keep the bowler, release and ball unobstructed
- Do not cut cameras
- Do not show popups
- Do not animate the HUD
- Do not use excessive motion blur
- Do not add particles near the ball
- Do not sacrifice visibility for cinematic presentation

Persistent information should sit quietly at the periphery:

- Score
- Ball and over
- Pitch
- Bowler
- Assistance
- Compact field radar

After the ball is dead, the interface may explain and present the consequence.

FIELD INTELLIGENCE

The player must understand the complete field before beginning.

Show:

- All eleven fielders
- Named positions
- Batter orientation
- Boundary
- Attacking fielders
- Protected zones
- Available gaps
- Tactical field intention

Maintain a compact radar during play. After every shot, show how the ball path interacted with the field.

Demonstrate:

Allow repetition with progressively reduced assistance:

1. Full trajectory and ghost movement
2. Pitch and contact markers
3. Contact cue only
4. No assistance

Do not call a delivery mastered after one lucky result. Require repeatable execution.

FORM MEANS REAL IMPROVEMENT

Measure:

- Line and length recognition
- Pace recognition
- Swing and seam recognition
- Cut recognition
- Spin recognition
- Bounce judgment
- Footwork selection
- Timing consistency
- Bat-face control
- Shot direction
- Field awareness
- Running decisions

Form is a report of demonstrated skill.

Form must never secretly:

- Improve contact
- Widen timing windows
- Reduce movement
- Weaken the bowler
- Move fielders
- Change outcomes

A player improves because the player learned—not because a progression system increased hidden attributes.

EMOTIONAL AUTHENTICITY

Use the emotional rhythm observed in real high-pressure cricket:

- Concentration before release
- Anticipation during the approach
- Collective breath while the ball travels
- Contact response
- Rising expectation near the boundary
- Relief after survival
- Shock after a wicket
- Frustration after a missed opportunity
- Controlled celebration after four
- Full release after six
- Silence after a damaging dismissal
- Reflection before the next ball

Crowd, commentary and haptics react to completed physical events and match context. They never influence outcomes.

Do not scale the system to every bowler, pitch and variation until this vertical slice meets the visual, control, coaching and performance standards.

MEASUREMENT

Create a Real-Match Fidelity Score:

- Ball perception: 20
- Player control and agency: 20
- Cricket decision authenticity: 15
- Field and condition awareness: 10
- Player and equipment realism: 10
- Audio, crowd and haptics: 10
- Coaching and learning value: 10
- Continuity and absence of friction: 5

Release target: at least 90/100.

No category may compensate for a failure in ball visibility, control or fairness.

VALIDATION

Do not declare completion from code inspection, screenshots or automated tests alone.

Test with:

- Experienced cricket players
- Casual cricket followers
- Mobile gamers unfamiliar with batting technique

Success requires:

- At least 90% understand the setup and score.
- At least 90% feel their input controlled the shot.
- At least 85% correctly identify broad line and length.
- At least 80% recognize major delivery movement after training.
- At least 90% understand why they succeeded or failed after Coach Mode.
- At least 80% improve against the repeated delivery within five attempts.
- No tester believes the outcome was scripted.
- No tester reports that the interface obscured the ball.
- Experienced cricket players rate perceptual, tactical and emotional fidelity at least 9/10.
- Supported low-tier Android devices maintain stable 30 FPS.
- Capable devices maintain a 60 FPS target.
