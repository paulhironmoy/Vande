# Target Chase: the rules, the seeds and the targets

Owner: CHASE-CORE (the Chase logic of the 2026-11-08 program). Code: `src/practice/chase/**`, tests
`tests/unit/practice/chase/**`, the table tool `scripts/chase-targets.mjs`. Decision: `docs/VANDE-PRODUCT-SEQUENCE.md`
section 8, row 2026-11-08 08:09Z. The screens are `src/practice/ui/chase/**` (CHASE-UI); the words are COPY's.

A Target Chase is two six-ball overs (12 legal balls) of the ordinary Practice innings, one wicket, and a **target**: the
runs to win. It adds no physics, no second engine and no help. Every ball is bowled and judged by the frozen simulation
(sim v3, amendment 5f); the chase counts what the physics decided.

## Rules (`rules.ts`)

- The chase is won by the ball that takes the runs to the target or past it, and that ball ends it.
- It is lost by a wicket (`lost_wicket`, also on the 12th ball: the wicket is what ended it) or when the 12th ball has been
  bowled and the runs are one short or fewer (`lost_balls`). There is no tie: reaching the target exactly wins.
- There are no extras: Practice bowls no wides and no no-balls (`docs/PRACTICE_RULES.md`), so every ball counts.
- A wicket ball scores nothing, so a wicket and a win never happen on the same ball.
- Leaving a chase is `abandoned`: neither a win nor a loss, and nothing is saved as a result.
- "Practice this ball" is off while a chase is in progress and "Restart over" is never available (`chaseAllowed`). After the
  chase has ended the deciding ball can be watched and practised on its own; nothing done there changes the result.
- Nothing adapts: the same seed, the same difficulty and the same swings always give the same chase, ball for ball. The
  difficulty is chosen once, before the first ball. The bowling, the fields and the target never change with the score.

## Seeds and targets (`seeds.ts`, `targets.ts`)

A chase seed is a name from a fixed pool of 256 (`c000` to `c255`). A seed gives the 12 balls (two Random Overs of Club
bowling on a Balanced pitch, made by the seeded generator: `chaseOver`, `chaseOvers`) and, with the difficulty (`easy`,
`standard`, `hard`; Standard by default), the target from a table (over the 256 seeds: Easy 7 to 30, median 8; Standard 10 to 34,
median 12; Hard 11 to 40, median 18). **The 12 balls of a seed are the same at every
difficulty; only the target changes.**

The table is worked out in advance, not on the phone: one seed costs about 14 s of single-core desktop work on this Mac (median: the
coach reading the 12 balls about 10 s, the reference batter through the physics about 3.4 s; not measured on a phone). `targets.ts` is generated by
`scripts/chase-targets.mjs` and carries the digest of its numbers; do not edit it by hand. The method, the distribution of the
targets and what was and was not measured are in `TARGET-METHOD.md` (evidence folder
`release/product-sequence/stage-3-chase/CHASE-CORE/`).

**The reference batter** (`derive.ts`, `reference.ts`) is a simulated batter who faces the 12 balls. On each ball he picks
one of the responses the coach offers for it (recommended, safest, higher-scoring) from where the chase stands, with the
best chance of reaching the target (a finite dynamic program, exact because the balls are independent), and he plays it
with a timing error that is a Gaussian of 20 ms (the repository's expert-timing model), integrated at 24 fixed points (no
random number). A tier's target is worked out in three steps (policy `best-play-timing-20` version 2):

1. the largest target he still reaches at least 80% (Easy), 60% (Standard) or 25% (Hard) of the time, not below the tier's
   floor (7, 10, 14 runs; ruling R-34);
2. the **minimum chance** (ruling R-44): where step 1 would leave him with less than 10% (`MIN_CHANCE`), the target is the
   largest one he reaches at least 10% of the time instead, but never below 7 (`MIN_TARGET`, 6 + 1: a ball scores at most six).
   Over the 256 seeds this changes six Hard targets (c011 14 to 13, c021 14 to 13, c025 14 to 11, c136 14 to 10 and then 11 by
   step 3, c169 14 to 12, c229 14 to 13) and nothing else: the other 762 targets and chances are identical to version 1;
3. the **order rule**: the three tiers of a seed never share a number, so a tier that steps 1 and 2 left no higher than the
   easier one is put one above it. Only c136 Hard needs it (10 would equal its Standard 10, so it is 11), and there the
   reference batter reaches the target 4.3% of the time, below the 10% line: the one target of the table that he does.

Everything is derived from the seed alone, never from anyone's play.

What this does **not** say: his success rates are facts about a simulated batter, at assist `play`, right-handed, on a
device with no extra timing tolerance. How often a person reaches a target is UNPROVEN until people have played it; the
Learn and Real assist levels, a left-handed batter and a device in the Assisted tier all change the odds. A batter with
typical timing (40 ms) who still plays the best response on every ball reaches the Easy, Standard and Hard targets 43%, 24% and 6%
of the time (78 seeds that kept that table; TARGET-METHOD.md has the numbers and the ceiling table).

## Using it (`CHASE-API.md` in the program folder has the long form)

```ts
const def = makeChase(pickChaseSeed(Math.random()), 'standard');   // ChaseDefinition | null
let innings = startChaseInnings(def, { id, assist, handedness });  // over 1 chosen; ball 1 is next
const state = chaseFromInnings(def, innings);                      // ChaseState: runs, ballsLeft, runsNeeded, result ...
const step = chaseStep(def, innings);                              // bowl | wait | next_over | choose_over | finished
innings = chaseInningsReducer(def, innings, action);               // the one way to apply an innings action in a chase
const situation = chaseSituation(state);                           // text-free numbers and codes for HUD, audio, Unity, copy
```

The innings stays the one saved truth (with its leave-on-resume protection); the chase is read from its records, so the
two cannot drift. `resumeChase` checks a saved chase ball by ball. `chaseBridgeBlock(state)` gives the six figures the
Unity bridge carries. `history.ts` is the local history (the last result for each seed and difficulty, at most 32, nothing
counted, nothing sent).

## Regenerating and checking the table

```
node scripts/chase-targets.mjs derive --from 0 --to 16 --out <dir>      # 16 seeds, about 3.5 min; run under nice
node scripts/chase-targets.mjs table --in <dir> --write                  # all 256 files -> src/practice/chase/targets.ts
node scripts/chase-targets.mjs check --seed c012                         # works a seed out again and compares it
node scripts/chase-targets.mjs validate --in <dir> --seed c012 --out f   # plays his choices with random timing errors
node scripts/chase-targets.mjs converge --in <dir> --seed c012           # more timing-error points: do the answers move?
```

The per-seed files keep the coach's options and what each does over the 24 timing errors, so changing a share, a floor or the
minimum chance (`TIER_SHARE`, `TIER_FLOOR`, `MIN_CHANCE` in `derive.ts`) needs no new physics: run `table` again (it lists every
entry that differs from the table it replaces; files written under policy version 1 are valid for version 2, see
`DERIVATION_POLICY_VERSIONS`). Changing the reference timing (`REFERENCE_SIGMA_MS`) or the physics needs `derive` again (the
coach part is kept). After a new table: `node scripts/chase-targets.mjs min-chance-fixture --in <dir> --out
tests/unit/practice/chase/fixtures/min-chance-seeds.json` and `node scripts/chase-golden.mjs generate --out
tests/unit/practice/chase/fixtures/golden.json` (the golden hashes cover the chase definition, which carries the policy
version, so every hash moves when the version does).

## Known limits

- The pool is 256 seeds. Any other seed has no target (`makeChase` returns null); working one out takes seconds on a
  desktop and is for tools and tests only.
- The reference batter knows the 12 balls in advance, has expert timing and chooses perfectly from the coach's three
  responses; a person has none of the three. Standard and Hard are therefore harder for a person than their labels say; the
  reference timing, the shares, the floors and the minimum chance are the tuning knobs (`derive.ts`).
- Every target of the table is reached by the reference batter at least 10% of the time except c136 Hard (11 runs, 4.3%; the
  order rule, step 3 above). The lowest Standard chance is 13.7% (c025) and the lowest Easy chance 25.0% (c025).
- A left-handed batter gets the mirror image of the right-hander's chase: `chaseOver` flips every bowler's arm, and the
  physics flips it again for a left-handed striker, so each ball plays out exactly as for a right-hander with the same swing
  (same runs, wickets, contact and field; tested ball by ball). Without the flip a left-hander faced a different physical
  problem and the same target was anywhere from 4% to 99% of the reference batter's innings. The bowlers a left-hander sees
  are therefore mostly left-arm (about 80% of the draws are right-arm for a right-hander). Always take the plan from
  `chaseOver`, never from `randomOver`.
